Jewrist

Kosher Tourism App · 2022

Jewish people who keep the laws of 'Kosher' find a hard time when it comes to planning a new trip — is there kosher food, restaurants, hotels, and apartments in the destination?

Solution: An app that gathers all the correct and accurate information about Kosher resources almost all over the world.

❤️ Empathize — Survey & Interview

I assumed kosher travelers would look for kosher restaurants, shops, and accommodations. A survey posted on Facebook and WhatsApp groups revealed a different picture.

Survey findings: Most respondents travel at least once a year. Kosher restaurants and kosher hotels are equally popular. People prefer destinations with a Jewish community or Bet Chabad. Kosher vacations are usually not organized by a single app. People are not very interested in local Jewish-history sites.

Key features requested: Price comparisons and user ratings / Kosher restaurants by destination / Kosher product photos and kashrut info / Filtering restaurants by location / Barcode scanning for kosher products

🎤 Interviews

I conducted interviews with kosher observant people who travel abroad with their families. Beyond the quantitative survey data, I gained qualitative and in-depth information — a better understanding of users' pain points.

Conclusions: Need a list of kosher products (including unmarked ones) / Kosher restaurants list / Small private places and B&Bs / Community-updatable content / Save for later functionality

🎯 Define — Persona, User Story & User Journey

💡 Ideate — Competitive Analysis, User Flow & Wireframes

📱 Prototype, UI & User Testing #1

Problems found in testing: Users didn't understand the map/list view toggle. The 'to-stay' & 'to-eat' tabs were confused with planning/saving. Users wanted to see both accommodations and restaurants together.

Key insight: Remove the map/list toggle buttons. Consolidate to 3 tabs.

ZOOM For Orthodox

Feature Redesign · 2022

A redesign of the ZOOM screen-sharing feature for better accessibility — aimed at Orthodox Jewish users who usually don't speak English but use ZOOM heavily for learning and Torah study.

● Problem

The English-only interface caused fear and confusion. Users were afraid of accidentally sharing private content instead of relevant documents.

🔍 Research — Empathize & Define

User interviews revealed that ultra-Orthodox students love ZOOM — it saves them time and money. The main pain point was screen sharing: unclear options caused anxiety about accidentally sharing personal information.

Opportunities found: More visual explanations / Onboarding animations / Hebrew language support / Clearer Cancel button

📊 Competitive Analysis

All competitors use informative icons and divide options into clear sections. Adopted: guest status indicators, subtitles, illustrative icons, Hebrew support, reducible self-view panel.

🕸 Wireframes — Lo-Fi & Hi-Fi

Language barrier → added illustrations and icons. Fear of sharing files → moved Cancel button to a more prominent position. Many sharing options → divided into two vertical columns to reduce cognitive load.

🎨 UI Design + 2nd Usability Testing

Designed following ZOOM's visual guidelines. Key elements: warning message, informative illustration, actions split into 2 sections (device + external apps), bold Cancel button.

🎉 Results

91% success rate — after the final iteration, participants understood the screen-sharing option, their self-confidence grew, and overall satisfaction increased significantly.

My Story

Benny Unger — Product Builder (Senior Product Designer)

I started with a paintbrush. Fine art, illustration — the kind of work where you stare at something for three hours and no one tells you to ship it.

Then I discovered product design, and something clicked: the real craft isn't making things beautiful — it's making them work. The visual skills I built didn't disappear, but they became a tool in service of that, not the point.

I'm a senior product designer who leads with UX. Functionality, clarity, how people actually use things — that's where I start. The art background earns its place when it helps communicate a flow, make a pattern legible, or reduce cognitive load. Not before.

Lately I've been deep in AI + product design. I use Claude Code to ship directly from Figma — this portfolio is one of them. Something clicked when I realized I could have a conversation with my tools and they'd build things back. That's not automation. That's collaboration.

I'm building in public. If you're curious about the intersection of design craft and AI, I'm probably already thinking about what you're thinking about.
